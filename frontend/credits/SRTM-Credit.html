SRTM 30m Global Elevation

NASA / NGA Mission 1 Arc-Second (~30m) InSAR Topography

Provenance & Attribution

The digital elevation model layers served in this interface originate from the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM), flown aboard the Space Shuttle Endeavour (STS-99) in February 2000.

This dataset is a collaborative effort led by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA), and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).

NASA Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) (2013). Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) Global 1 arc second. Distributed by OpenTopography / USGS Earth Resources Observation and Science (EROS) Center.

Engineering Usage Notes

Vertical Precision & Limitations

SRTM data is an interferometric C-band radar product reflecting the reflective phase center of the surface (vegetation canopy, dense urban structures, and bare earth combined).

  • Catchment & Route Corridor Scoping: Recommended for watershed delineation, preliminary alignment corridors, regional geomorphology, and preliminary grade planning.
  • Not for Final Structural Design: Due to vertical deviations caused by canopy penetration limits and steep terrain shadowing/void filling, this product does not substitute terrestrial leveling, certified total station surveys, or high-density LiDAR.
  • Datum Context: Horizontal datum is WGS84; vertical elevations are referenced to the WGS84/EGM96 geoid.
